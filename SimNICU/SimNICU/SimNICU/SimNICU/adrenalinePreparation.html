A) Cardiac arrest (using 100 micrograms/ml strength):

Only to be given when a doctor/ANNP is in attendance

Intravenous administration preferably into an umbilical venous catheter, long line or Broviac line, although administration via a peripheral IV cannula is also acceptable. Fast bolus administration followed by a 1 ml sodium chloride 0.9% flush.

ETT - place a catheter to lie just beyond the end of the ET tube, then fast bolus administration followed by a 0.5-1ml sodium chloride 0.9% flush.
 

B) Hypotension (using 1mg/ml strength)

Must be administered via a central line

  1. 3 x weight (kg) = number of mg adrenaline needed. 
  2. Divide this number by 1 to give the number of mls of adrenaline 1mg in 1ml solution needed  
  3. Make the calculated volume of adrenaline up to 50ml with dextrose 5%, dextrose 10%, sodium chloride 0.18%/dextrose 10% or sodium chloride 0.9%. 

Then 1ml/hr = 1 microgram/kg/minute

The quantity of drug can be doubled in fluid restricted patients i.e. use 6mg/kg of adrenaline 1mg in 1ml solution. Then 1ml/hr = 2 micrograms/kg/minute. The final concentration should ideally not exceed 0.32mg/ml although could be used more concentrated or even neat if fluid restricted (although there is little evidence for this).
